Disarray is a heavyweight robot built by Wildcat Robotics, formerly known as Team 22037, which has competed at BattleBots Proving Ground and FaceOffs. It is also an instated alternate for the 2026 BattleBots Pro League.

It is a four-wheeled, cyan and black robot armed with a large, belt-driven drisk weapon at the front powered by a brushless NeuMotor. According to the team, their weapon is capable of spinning at up to 250mph, and weighs up to 50lbs (originally 45lbs).[1] Disarray also features a dedicated self-righting mechanism towards the back. The overall design of Disarray resembles a stingray, evidenced by its large curved wedges protruding from its sides and resembling fins. Wildcat Robotics, made up of University of Arizona students, began work on Disarray in the fall of 2022 as the team's university Capstone project, and uploaded an application video to World Championship VIII in May 2023.[2][3]

According to an article by EI Inde, immediately had plans to enter BattleBots Proving Ground, a part of the live show with a focus on teams trying out new designs, hoping to then be accepted for World Championship VIII at a later time.[1] The team then confirmed they would compete on the weekend of May 6-7, 2023, and was scheduled to face Pteradominator.[4] It took the win in both fights, finishing its first weekend of fights with an unbeaten 2-0 record. Disarray returned in September 2023 for another pair of Proving Ground fights.[5]

In the months since its previous fights, Wildcat Robotics swapped out Disarray's batteries and speed controllers. It also now sported mounting points for forks on the front wedges and a tail which doubled up as a self-righting mechanism. Accompanying Disarray was a 12lb, flame-throwing minibot called Heatwave. Its scheduled opponent was a fellow rookie in Taco Tuesday, who it defeated in its opening fight to extend its run to 3-0. This was ended in their second encounter of the weekend, however, when Disarray's self-righting mechanism failed after being flipped over. Disarray returned again in late October and fought a Top 32 finisher and Golden Bolt qualifier in JackPot, losing both matches.

Disarray was scheduled to fight Orbitron across the weekend of March 9-10, 2024. In January, Al Hurworth confirmed Disarray would be rebuilt from the ground up ahead of its 2024 battles and feature no shared parts with its 2023 build.[6] Among its upgrades were a more compact design, thicker ¼" steel armor, new batteries and repositioning the weapon belts to the outside of the weapon in order to allow for a single disk weapon option.[2] Parts for this rebuild arrived in February, and Disarray was ready for its March fights. However, its winless streak stretched to five following two losses by knockout to Orbitron during the Saturday and Sunday shows.

Disarray fought three more times during BattleBots FaceOffs in October 2024.[7] Ahead of the competition, the robot was again fully redesigned, now sporting a large plow protecting its front wheels and a single disk configuration.[8] Its armor was heavier still, with the team able to opt for a maximum thickness of ⅜".

Returning for the August 2025 'BattleBots: Next Generation' FaceOffs group, a stronger self-righting arm and brand new livery were two of its upgrades. Their minibot - a repurposed RC monster truck - sported a flamethrower.

Disarray was originally an alternate for the 2026 BattleBots Pro League, as confirmed by the team.[9] Since their last outing, the team had new urethane wheels made, as well as long front forks and greater weapon reach. The team had focused on practicing driving with magnets now fitted to improve downforce. It would be drafted into Group B to replace Claw Viper, adopting its fight schedule.

Outside BattleBots[]

Disarray was on display during Bash at the REC on August 20, 2023, a campus event held for students of The University of Arizona.

The team was also registered for RoboGames 2024, but withdrew in March that year. The team would host a competition called Wildcat Robotics Sonoran Showdown on March 23, 2024.

Disarray at Bash at the REC.

Robot History[]

BattleBots Proving Ground[]

Disarray vs. Pteradominator (5/7/2023)

Disarray's first ever BattleBots fight came in BattleBots Proving Ground, where it took on Pteradominator. For this fight, Disarray lacked a self-righting mechanism, making it vulnerable to flipping attacks.

As Pteradominator dashed forward, the more nimble Disarray manoeuvred around and pointed its weapon at its opponent. Disarray then slammed into Pteradominator, turning it over in the first seven seconds, giving it a significant upper hand as it pushed it into the corner.
"OHHH, big hit from Disarray there!"
— Bil Dwyer as Disarray flips Pteradominator

Disarray then lined up a large hit onto the side of Pteradominator, causing several batteries to be ejected from the bot and severely crippling its movement.


"Oh my gosh... what's this? There's something flying around the arena there"
— Steve Judkins as Pteradominator's internal components begin shorting

The countdown started on Pteradominator, but with four seconds remaining, it was able to show slight movement from one side so the fight was allowed to continue. Disarray came in one last time and bumped into Pteradominator without its weapon moving. This move fully incapacitated Pteradominator, and a fresh count began.

Disarray took the win by knockout in 1:03, marking its debut with a win.[10]

Disarray vs. Pteradominator (5/8/2023)

The following day, Disarray took on Pteradominator for a second time. On this occasion, Pteradominator opted to lead with its spinning weaponry.

Both robots began the fight in the blue square, where Pteradominator's flipper was slightly ajar and its spinner got up to speed. The same could not be said for Disarray, however, whose weapon struggled to power up. Both bots went weapon-to-weapon and sparks flew, but Disarray quickly asserted its superior drive power and rammed Pteradominator into the housing for the screws.

Pteradominator escaped from danger, but Disarray gave chase and shoved it into the opposite screws, buckling the front of Pteradominator. Though Disarray's weapon was no longer spinning, it still proved capable of damaging its opponent, who could now only drive in arcs on the edge of the red square with its weapon also disabled.
"Disarray... having its way."
— Bil Dwyer as Disarray asserts its dominance

Though parts began to come off Disarray, these were purely cosmetic and the Arizona team continued their assault on Pteradominator. But with a minute to go, Pteradominator appeared high-centered as its wheels spun but the robot could no longer move. A count began and Disarray took the win by knockout to finish the weekend undefeated.

Disarray vs. Taco Tuesday (9/16/2023)

Disarray returned to the BattleBox in September 2023, where it fought Taco Tuesday.

In the first exchange, Disarray tore both right side weapon pulleys from Taco Tuesday's weapon, leaving it at half power. Taco Tuesday retaliated by cutting into the left fin of Disarray before being driven into the short corner. Here, it was hit by the pulverizer and struggled to escape until almost a minute into the fight. Disarray then pinned Taco Tuesday against the paddle, getting its drisc underneath the shell of Carter Hurd's robot.

Taco Tuesday escaped again and struck Disarray's minibot. However, it was punted across the BattleBox floor several times in the second half of the battle. Taco Tuesday would remain stuck in the corner unable to move out of it for much of the last minute of the fight, also losing its remaining weapon belts and ending the fight without a working spinner.

The fight went the distance, with Disarray picking up the win by crowd vote, extending its career record to 3-0.

Disarray vs. Taco Tuesday (9/17/2023)

Disarray took on Taco Tuesday for a second time during the weekend. Ahead of the fight, Team Wildcat were experiencing issues with their weapon. Though this was eventually resolved, the team ran out of time to test their self-righter.[11]

In the first exchange, Disarray would get around to Taco Tuesday's side and landed a series of blows that turned Taco Tuesday over. Taco Tuesday would quickly rebound due to its geometry.

Finding its footing, Taco Tuesday would engage in a weapon-to-weapon collision with a charging Disarray, flipping it over. After not having had time to test their self-righting tail, this mechanism failed and Disarray could not recover from being upside-down.[12]

Taco Tuesday went after Disarray's minibot, before trying to right the main robot. This effort proved futile and Disarray was counted out, ending the rookie team's unbeaten streak.

Disarray vs. JackPot (10/28/2023)

Returning on the weekend of October 28-29, 2023, Disarray took on Las Vegas locals JackPot. For these fights, the team had worked on their self-righter and ensured it had sufficient torque to right their robot if flipped.

Disarray drove to the middle of the arena as JackPot approached with its lifter raised. The rookie bot backed up slightly before going weapon-to-weapon with the Sin City Slugfest finalist.

These impacts cost Disarray several weapon belts and as it looked to recover, JackPot delivered a follow-up shot to its left side, tearing off the fin and leaving a gash in Disarray's weapon. Parts littered the BattleBox floor as Disarray was thrown back on impact, landing inverted against the Upper Deck.

Though Disarray was able to prove its self-righter could work, it lost all mobility and began smoking as it was counted out. JackPot ultimately won by knockout in under a minute.

Disarray vs. JackPot (10/29/2023)

Rebuilt ahead of this fight, Disarray took on JackPot for a second time during the Sunday show.

This time, Disarray showed more hesitation in landing an early hit as JackPot also remained patient. Jeff Waters' robot then met Disarray head-on, ripping into its side fin.

JackPot delivered another hit to the front of Disarray, throwing it upside-down. The drisc of Disarray dragged it back towards the short corner before it slammed into the Upper Deck. Disarray was again able to showcase its self-righting mechanism as it hoisted itself over.

However, JackPot was lurking and struck the rear of Disarray, tearing its back panel and hitting its lithium polymer batteries. As thick, white smoke began to pour from Disarray, it was counted out and finished the weekend winless.

Disarray vs. Orbitron (3/9/2024)

Disarray next took on fellow rookies in Orbitron across the weekend of March 9-10, 2024. For this fight, Disarray was accompanied by an off-the-shelf remote-control monster truck which they named Shark Bait. The purpose of the truck was to disrupt Orbitron's AI system.

Disarray began this fight on the back foot as Orbitron's new forks helped drive it into the corner. The fight moved to the middle of the arena and as Disarray looked to retaliate, Orbitron charged with its rear disc and flung Disarray over, ripping off its right-front wheel and disabling that side of drive in the process.

Although its left-side drive was still functional, Disarray was unable to right itself from its inverted position and was counted out in under a minute.

Disarray vs. Orbitron (3/10/2024)

Disarray was scheduled to fight Orbitron for a second time the following day.

Disarray drove at its opponent and turned, but offered its flank to Orbitron in the first ten seconds. Orbitron tore off a fin and removed Disarray's left-rear tire. Obitron then led with its forks and drove Disarray into the corner, throwing it upside-down.

With the main robot's self-righting mechanism failing, Shark Bait attempted to distract Orbitron, but suffered a hit which shattered its plastic body. Disarray was nudged back into the action by the paddle hazard, but this only served to tee the Arizona representatives up for one final shot from Orbitron's rear blade.

As Disarray was counted out, Orbitron again turned its attention to Shark Bait and tore its body off completely before removing a wheel. A knockout was declared and Disarray's winless weekend was confirmed.

BattleBots FaceOffs[]

Disarray vs. JackPot

Disarray competed at one of eight mini tournaments in Fall 2024, known as BattleBots FaceOffs. The first of its three battles was a rematch against JackPot - a robot Disarray had lost to twice previously. Pre-battle, Al Hurworth explained that Disarray had undergone numerous modifications since their last meetings, now on "version three" of their machine.

As the fight commenced, Disarray avoided an early attack to its back end early by pivoting around to face its opponent. However, Disarray then oversteered, driving into JackPot's vertical spinner and causing one of its top panels to come away. JackPot's single disk buffeted against the front of Disarray, which became stuck momentarily on its opponent's minibot, Ace.

Disarray escaped, but JackPot was keen to re-engage and met the Wildcat Robotics machine with a frontal attack. Minibot Ace drove around to high-center Disarray for a second time, allowing JackPot to target the defenceless back end of Disarray.

Soon after, Disarray's primary weapon abruptly stopped as JackPot remained the aggressor. The Las Vegas machine next ripped the other half of Disarray's top armor off, exposing its internals before slamming into the front and then the flank of Al Hurworth's robot. As it inched back, JackPot charged once more and tore a chunk out of Disarray's front-right wheel and dislodged a drive chain, further hampering its mobility.
"Ohhh, that looks like it hurt!"
— Steve Judkins as Disarray suffers another damaging attack from JackPot
Disarray could only twitch in place due to the accumulation of damage, so a count began, meaning Wildcat Robotics began their FaceOffs campaign with a loss by knockout.

Disarray vs. Malice

Disarray next fought Malice, an experienced veteran and their first opponent armed with a horizontal spinner. In response, Disarray equipped a 35lb front wedge to the robot, which captain Al Hurworth confirmed was designed based on Jackpot's own wedge, with input from Jeff Waters. The team also printed an "engagement ring" washer for their opponents, to prevent Malice's weapon shaft from getting caught in the hellraisers.

Disarray was tentative in the opening seconds of the match, letting Malice make the first approach and first strike. This opening attack damaged Malice's own drive, however, leaving it vulnerable. Disarray attempted to press the attack, but Malice's forks kept it mostly at bay.
"A strategic game of chess going on here!"
— Steve Judkins calls attention to the slow pace of the fight

Disarray continued landing darting attacks, losing several armor panels but also managing to rip the tooth off of Malice's weapon in the process. However, its progress was constantly impeded by traction problems - its rear wheels were visually spinning freely at several points. The second minute of the fight was, as a result, rather quiet, with only a few glancing contacts between the robots.

"That's the way! The effective wedge."
— Bil Dwyer is excited when something finally happens
In the final minute of the fight, Disarray managed two ramming attacks which popped its opponent in the air, grinding against Malice's underside briefly before releasing. This proved enough to satisfy the audience, who judged Disarray the winner by crowd vote, improving it to 1-1 and finally ending its losing streak.
"We got the win. We've been on a, what, six match losing streak? So it feels so good- so good to win."
— Al Hurworth celebrates the end of Disarray's drought

The team would later reveal that the cause of their drive problems was the wedge: having done almost no testing with it equipped, they were unprepared for how much it threw off Disarray's balance, moving too much weight to the front and preventing their rear wheels from gaining traction through much of the match.

Disarray vs. Lucky

"So we're fighting Lucky tonight. We've never fought a flipper before, so it's going to be an interesting challenge."
— Al Hurworth

In its last FaceOffs battle of 2024, Disarray fought fellow Canadian representative and ABC/Discovery era veteran Lucky. Here, it opted for its split front wedge configuration as with its encounter with JackPot, and was fitted with a larger self-righting 'tail'. The new 'tail' was, according to Al Hurworth, designed to 'bow out to the sides', providing a larger surface area than the srimech used in previous fights. Hurworth added that the only other change Disarray received was 'a lot of electrical tuning', carried out by Wildcat Robotics earlier that morning. The tuning lead to Disarray demonstrating noticeably faster movements during a BattleBox test; to the extent that adjustments were suggested for its left-right transmitter channel.

"We just went in the Box. Weapon was looking good. Drive was really looking good today... fixed that after the Malice fight yesterday. It's zippin' around a lot better today. This, for us, is probably the most exciting fight of the weekend. All systems are lookin' really good. We've been tuning it all weekend. So really gettin' excited... and hopefully we can come out 2-1 today."
— Al Hurworth's view on Disarray's much improved drive and weapon responsiveness
Disarray, starting in the red square, quickly sped into the lower perimeter to avoid Lucky's initial charge. Turning around, it rammed Lucky's front-right corner, but without getting the bite to cause damage. As Lucky backed into the lower-left corner, Disarray followed. During a set of head-to-head exchanges, its spinner generated sparks from the front wedgelets of TKO Robotics' machine. The wedgelets, however, breached Disarray's front ground clearance, allowing Lucky to push and flip it a full 360 degrees onto the top-left Screws. Disarray became wedged for a few seconds as its right 'fin' rested against the BattleBox floor. Smoke rose as Disarray's rear wheels regained purchase, allowing it to back away speedily towards the top-left Killsaw slots.

A tentative stand-off saw Disarray shuffle, then circle around and into the front of Lucky. While it was pushed back towards the Upper Deck Screws, a bolt dropped out from beneath its front-left wedge section. The bolt was later confirmed by Al Hurworth to be from its spinner assembly; its removal reduced the weapon's effectiveness and caused the shaft to lower as the match progressed. Disarray churned away at Lucky's wedgelets once more, but was flung over a Screw bumper into the top-right corner. In the process, its spinner clipped the front of Lucky's flipper, bending it out of shape and preventing it from retracting.
Bil Dwyer: "Right over the edge! Like to see that."
Steve Judkins: "Disarray taking two big hits here - weapon still spinning! The boys have got to be happy with that."
Bil Dwyer: "Oh, but what we don't like to see, is Lucky's flipping arm, stuck in the up position!"
Steve Judkins: "Oh... that is not a good sign. Definitely makes him vulnerable. Disarray can go straight on now... into the mouth, almost!"
— As Disarray indirectly disables Lucky's flipper
Landing upright, Disarray bounced into a 180-degree spin into the top-right corner. Another tentative spell saw it turn and graze the bumper it flew over, before weaving into Lucky's front-right wedgelets. Disarray sent Lucky spinning away, and retreated to the lower-left Killsaw slots to spin its weapon back up. It shuffled and initially struggled to engage with an approaching Lucky. When both competitors next clashed opposite the control booth, Disarrray attacked Lucky's front-left wedgelets, while Lucky pushed it into the center blue square Screw mount. Disarray was pinned in place for a few seconds, before being shepherded into the lower-right corner.

Retreating across the lower perimeter, Disarray missed its next strike on Lucky's side armor. In the same turn, it again pitched its spinner into Lucky's front-right wedgelets. The collision popped Disarray into the air, resulting in it being hooked by the front-left wheel. Both machines pushed and dragged each other in circles over the BattleBox center. Having bent Lucky's right fork during this exchange, Disarray warped it further upon slamming into it a second time. The impact separated the two robots, though Disarray was slow to get away and its spinner had stopped rotating. In another tentative exchange, Disarray swiped Lucky with its left 'fin', then tried to push it from the front-right corner. When it next collided head-on with Lucky, it found itself clamped between Lucky's wedgelets and flipper. Disarray was pushed into the lower bumper, its 'fin' catching the Killsaw slots in the process.

As Disarray was being pinned and pushed around in circles, its spinner fired up once more. Disarray broke free when Lucky tried to shove it past the Killsaw slots, leading to another chase over the center as the match approached its final minute. A sluggish Disarray was rounded up and pushed forward by Lucky, leading to another head-to-head exchange where Disarray resumed its earlier attacks to Lucky's forks. This time, Disarray was pushed backwards into the lower-right corner, precariously close to the descending Pulverizer. Disarray bounced upwards as its spinner clipped Lucky's front-right armor panel, then its flipper arm from the same side. Another pushing match followed, ending with Lucky delivering Disarray into the lower-right Screw.

Disarray escaped to line up another attack to Lucky's front-right corner. Again, Lucky won out on traction and ground game, shoving Disarray into the inner-left Upper Deck Screw and the center bumper nearby. Disarray struggled to move away or position its spinner, as it found itself being pushed across the BattleBox center from the right-hand side. Another tentative stage ensued once Disarray broke away. Disarray landed a final series of hits to Lucky's front corners during the closing twenty seconds, with its spinner noticeably lacking torque by this stage. It and Lucky otherwise kept ramming each other, and finished the match locked together. Both survived to a judges' decision and crowd vote.
"So, we did some really good damage at the beginning. Bent their arm in so it couldn't go back down. But if we could just kinda get off of them a little bit, get our weapon spinning up full bore again, I think we really could've ended the fight there. I think we were really close. We disabled their flipper pretty fast, but they did kind of control us, you know, pushing us into the wall a few times. But, I mean we did have all of our systems fully functional at the end."
— Al Hurworth reflects on Disarray's damage output

When the audience gave equal cheers to Disarray and Lucky, Steve Judkins subsequently confirmed that Lucky was deemed to be the match winner. Regardless of the more noticeable damage Disarray delivered, Al Hurworth conceded that Lucky was more controlling. Hurworth - satisfied with his robot's improving performance - proceeded to congratulate and pat Mark Demers on the back for a 'great fight'. With this outcome, both of their robots finished FaceOffs Group B joint second with Malice, all with 1-2 match records apiece.

"I think we did great today. This is our eleventh fight, and it's the best our systems ever, ever worked."
— Al Hurworth

BattleBots Pro League (2026)[]

Disarray vs. Magnitude

Having been instated into the Pro League in place of Claw Viper, Disarray adopted its fight schedule and the first of its three group opponents was Magnitude. For this battle, Disarray was equipped with its long, thin forks.

Disarray began by exiting its blue square, but hesitating as Magnitude approached with its weapon already up to speed. By contrast, Disarray's drisk weapon was not spinning and an early assault tore away both forks from Al Hurworth's machine. Disarray was therefore stripped of its ground game just ten seconds into this group battle and threw a weapon belt moments later. However, its spinner then got up to speed.

Disarray stumbled around in the corner before the bots faced each other, Magnitude landed two hits, first popping up Disarray, then launching it several feet into the air. The bots engaged once again, popping each other back before Magnitude capitalized upon Disarray's drive problems, ripping off its cosmetic side fin.

The all-out assault continued as Magnitude landed multiple hits before Disarray threw itself upside-down. Magnitude capitalized on this by landing a hit while Disarray was trying to self-right, then opting to show mercy as its opponent was barely able to move. Chad New's team asked Wildcat Robotics if they wanted to continue, seeking affirmation before advancing again.

Magnitude launched Disarray into the screws, briefly getting the self-righter of Disarray stuck in the hazard. Disarray became free but Magnitude fiercely sent its opponent violently up and into the arena wall.

Disarray landed flat on its back, with the self-righter stuck in place as Magnitude retreated to the center of the arena. David Sanfilippo liaised with Disarray's team and a count began soon after, confirming its opening round defeat by knockout.

Trivia[]

  • Disarray underwent nineteen different designs as of March 2023, with one of the earliest coming in at around 380lbs.
  • Disarray was originally designed under the working name Manta.
    • Disarray then applied for World Championship VIII under the name Sting, but the team were encouraged to change their name by Greg Munson.[13]
  • The RC monster truck seen in Disarray's fights against Orbitron was actually an off-the-shelf 1:15 scale toy of the Monster Jam truck Megalodon.
  • Disarray's plow introduced for BattleBots FaceOffs was originally designed by Jeff Waters for JackPot and lent to them ahead of their September 2024 fights.
